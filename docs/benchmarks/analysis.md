# Van Roy baseline — analysis & hotspots

_Companion to [`baseline.md`](baseline.md), which is auto-regenerated by the
harness and only holds numbers. This file is hand-curated; the harness does
not touch it._

_Based on the 2026-06-02 5-runs-per-cell measurement on
`GUSBRO-NB`, Intel 8-core, Windows 10 LTSC, .NET 10._

## Summary

| Comparison | Geometric mean of ratios | Best | Worst |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Shumway / GProlog native | **~13.6×** slower | zebra (8.5×) | serialize (25.5×) |
| Shumway / SWI interpreted | **~3.0×** slower | serialize (2.2×) | boyer (7.7×) |

GProlog and SWI themselves differ by ~5× in their favour, which is the
canonical interpretation-vs-native-compile gap. Shumway is currently
~3× behind SWI's bytecode interpreter — reasonable for a young project,
but with identifiable improvement targets below.

## Per-benchmark observations

### Strong cases for Shumway

- **`zebra` (8.5× vs gprolog)** — Shumway's closest match to native
  compile. Pure structural backtracking with no arithmetic. Our
  chunk-127+ in-place dynamic dispatch and trail machinery hold up
  well; the gap is the constant-factor cost of bytecode dispatch vs
  inlined machine code.

- **`serialize` (2.2× vs SWI)** — best ratio against SWI. Term
  construction + BST traversal exercises our heap GC (ADR-016) and
  unification path, both well-tuned.

### Hotspots — concrete leads

- **`serialize` (25.5× vs gprolog)** — the *worst* gap vs native,
  despite being our best vs SWI. The reading: gprolog's native compile
  of this exact pattern (heap allocation + tree walk) is just
  exceptionally good — it runs in 4.2 µs/iter, vs SWI's 48 µs (11×
  ratio between them). Not a Shumway-specific weakness, more a ceiling
  imposed by interpretation.

- **`crypt` (23.3× vs gprolog)** and **`queens` (21.5× vs gprolog)** —
  the two arithmetic-heavy workloads. gprolog compiles `is/2` to direct
  machine instructions; we dispatch through the bytecode interpreter's
  arithmetic evaluator + `Number` type. Inlining hot arithmetic in
  Tier-1 IL would close most of this gap — Phase 16's threaded IL
  dispatch already handles the call path, but the arithmetic
  evaluation itself is still bytecode-only.

- **`boyer` (7.7× vs SWI)** — the only benchmark above the SWI mean of
  ~3×. Boyer drives heavy `=../2`-based term reflection. SWI has a
  highly optimised C-level `=..`; our implementation does a full
  AST↔heap round-trip. Specialising `=..` for the common case
  (functor + args, known arity) is a clear win.

- **`tak` (14.4× vs gprolog, 3.7× vs SWI)** — the canonical
  recursion+arithmetic benchmark. Same story as queens/crypt — native
  arithmetic is hard to beat. Tail-call optimisation is fine on our
  side (chunk 110 confirmed LCO); the gap is purely per-call cost.

- **`nreverse` (13.1× vs gprolog, 2.8× vs SWI)** — heavy unification +
  cons-cell allocation. Allocation rate is the dominant factor; our
  heap GC (ADR-016) is competitive but not yet inline-optimised the
  way SWI's is for cons-cell hot paths.

## What this tells us about Shumway's invariants

- **Backtracking / cut machinery** is solid. zebra, sendmore, and
  queens all stay within the cluster's geometric mean. The chunk-118+
  in-place dynamic dispatch (ADR-015) is paying off.

- **Arithmetic** is the largest single deficit vs native gprolog.
  Three of the four worst ratios (queens, crypt, tak) are arithmetic-
  heavy. The bytecode evaluator was tuned for correctness, not for
  per-operation throughput.

- **`=..` and term reflection** are slow enough to dominate boyer.
  Specialised paths for common shapes would help.

- **No outlier-bad benchmark** vs SWI — every cell is within 2.2–7.7×.
  This means the engine doesn't have any "broken" workload; everything
  scales together with the dispatch-cost constant.

## What this is NOT yet

- No CPU-time measurement (only wall-clock) — process startup
  contaminates short workloads. We mitigate with the `bench(0)`
  startup subtraction, but for sub-millisecond per-iter benchmarks
  (boyer on gprolog) it bottoms out as `<noise>`.

- No memory tracking. Shumway is allocation-heavy in places; SWI uses
  a tagged-pointer scheme that avoids some of that. A future analysis
  pass should measure peak heap and GC frequency.

- One machine, one snapshot. Different CPUs (especially AMD vs Intel)
  shift the ratios non-trivially because gprolog's native code
  benefits from cache effects that the interpreted engines don't.

## Improvement targets, in priority order

1. **Inline arithmetic in Tier-1 IL** — `is/2`, `=:=/2`, `>/2` etc. with
   integer fast-paths. Targets crypt, queens, tak.
2. **`=..` specialisation** — fast path for `Term =.. [Functor|Args]`
   where Term is bound. Targets boyer.
3. **Heap-cell allocation fast-path** — inline cons-cell creation
   without going through the full ADR-016 GC barrier on the hot path.
   Targets nreverse, flatten, qsort.
4. **Unification specialisation** — list-pattern unification in Tier-1
   IL with no allocation for the result lists in deterministic cases.
   Targets serialize.

Each of these would close ~10–30% of the gap to SWI for its target
benchmarks. Closing the gap to native gprolog wholesale would require
a true machine-code backend, which is a much larger undertaking and
out of scope until at least Phase 27+.

## Reproducibility

Re-run with the same setup:

```
dotnet run -c Release --project tests/Shumway.Tests.Benchmarks/ -- --vanroy --runs 5
```

For quick spot-checks (drift from the baseline), `--runs 3` is enough
to wash out most noise and runs ~40% faster.
